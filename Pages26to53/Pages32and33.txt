STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "aandelig Forstand" – not merely intellectual understanding, but spiritually illumined discernment.
* "Aanden" – the Holy Spirit; must not be weakened to general spirituality.
* "Postiller" – sermon collections arranged by pericopes; not generic devotional books.
* "Homilier" – formal sermons with doctrinal exposition.
* "blaa Ænder" – idiomatic expression meaning trivialities or nonsense; metaphorical.
* "Ordning" – ecclesiastical order/arrangement; not mere organization.
* "Forfatning" – church polity/constitution, not political constitution in modern sense.
* "Salighedslære" – doctrine of salvation, with confessional precision.
* "Menighedsbegreb" – theological concept of congregation, not sociological community.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long hypotactic sentences with parenthetical clarifications.
* Rhetorical questions building cumulative pressure.
* Analogical chains (copper serpent, coin, shoes).
* Sermonic pacing that resists simplification.

Theological Hazards:

* Distinction between Spirit and human preaching.
* Authority and limits of church orders.
* Faith and love as governing norms.
* Apostolic doctrine and polity as inseparable.
* Urgency of salvation time (kairos theology).

Voice Hazards:

* Irony mixed with gravity.
* Earnest exhortation addressed to conscience.
* Polemical edge against papalism and complacency.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "prædiket om blaa Ænder" – idiom for preaching irrelevancies; must remain vivid, not paraphrased blandly.
* "brød istykker Kobberslangen" – 2 Kings 18:4; biblical precedent for abolishing misused divine institutions.
* "kjøbe den beleilige Tid" – Ephesians 5:16; redeeming the time.
* "nu er den velbehagelige Tid" – 2 Corinthians 6:2.
* Body-life analogy – Pauline ecclesiology (1 Corinthians 12).

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Thesis: Church orders exist solely to serve faith and love.
* Claim: When Spirit-led preaching is absent, fixed orders prevent arbitrariness.
* Warning: Orders become harmful when misused.
* Principle: Any order must be abolished once it damages faith.
* Analogical support: Copper serpent, debased coin, ill-fitting shoes.
* Doctrinal conclusion: Order has no intrinsic sanctity.
* Historical application: Lutheran reform incomplete in church polity.
* Rhetorical challenge: Is it un-Lutheran to question outward arrangements?
* Transition: From theory to practical work for living congregations.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* 2 Kings 18:4 – Copper serpent.
* 2 Corinthians 6:2 – Day of salvation.
* Ephesians 5:16 – Redeeming the time.
* Acts 2 – Outpouring of the Spirit.
* 1 Corinthians 12 – Body imagery.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Retain sharp evaluative language ("dead and gone").
* Preserve rhetorical questions without dilution.
* Maintain polemical firmness against sacralizing order.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* "maa" / "skal" rendered as "must" / "shall".
* Avoid weakening necessity into suggestion.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Aanden – the Spirit
* Ordning – order
* Menighed – congregation
* Tro – faith
* Kjærlighed – love
* Liv – life

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Keep concrete analogies intact.
* Do not explain metaphors away.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Irony → warning → exhortation → hope.
* Maintain shifts clearly.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences retained where argument accumulates.
* Semicolons preserved.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (VOICE-PRESERVING)

In any case, when spiritual understanding and the Spirit himself do not speak through the pastors (I do not mean by this to set limits on the Spirit; he knows well how to speak better than all postils and homilies), the result, nevertheless, is that each one preaches what he himself wishes, and then, instead of the Gospel and its exposition, one preaches about blue ducks. For one of the reasons why we retain the Epistles and the Gospels as they stand in the postils is this: that there are so few pastors of real spiritual depth who are able to treat an entire Evangelist or another biblical book with power and profit.

We may sum it up this way: this and every other form of order is to be used in such a way that wherever misuse arises from it, one abolishes it at once and introduces another, just as King Hezekiah broke in pieces and abolished the bronze serpent, which God himself had commanded to be made — because the children of Israel misused it. For orders exist to serve the advancement of faith and love and not to harm faith. When they no longer do this, then they are already dead and gone and no longer have any validity, just as when a good coin is debased, it is withdrawn and altered because of the misuse, or as when new shoes grow old and pinch, they are no longer worn, but are thrown away and others are bought. Order is an outward matter; be it ever so good, it can lead to misuse. But then it is no longer an order, but a disorder. Therefore no order has authority in itself, as papal orders have hitherto been regarded; but all the life, dignity, power, and virtue of an order lie in its right use; otherwise it is of no value or use at all. May God’s Spirit and grace be with us all. Amen.

Luther saw, of course, that if it was necessary to return to the apostolic doctrine of salvation, it was no less necessary to return to the apostolic understanding of the congregation and to the polity corresponding to it. The one must correspond to the other.

The misfortune is surely this: that the question of polity within the Lutheran Church has not kept pace with the question of doctrine as well as it did when Luther decisively rejected the doctrine and polity of the Catholic Church in the sixteenth century.

But is it then wrong to attempt now to do something that ought to have been done earlier? Or must one truly be so afraid of the purely outward aspect of the Church that one does not dare to risk placing a question mark at one point or another concerning the external order?

Is it to be established as an inviolable principle that if one has nothing to object to in the doctrine, then one must let what exists remain as it is?

Would that be Lutheran?

\subsubsection{A little about the work for living congregations}

One asks: how shall we go about working for the congregation in the biblical sense?

And naturally this is an important question in this connection, which demands an answer. We might indeed be inclined to add that one must first consider who is to do the work; but then we must take one thing at a time, and therefore just as well this matter of the work.

In the first place, then, we may be permitted to say that this work is of such a kind that it must be done continually and without interruption. Just as a human being labors for bodily life from cradle to grave, the whole body working for the sustenance and preservation of life, so the work for the life of the congregation and for a living congregation is an unceasing work from the time the Spirit was poured out until the coming of Christ. This work cannot all be done in a single effort; it must be carried on at all times. Life in the congregation must continually be nourished, and it has so many enemies that there must also be continual struggle in defense of life. The work is at all times so important for the congregation as a whole and for the individual souls that it is necessary always to keep in mind the apostolic word, that now is the acceptable time and today is the day of salvation, and that we must redeem the time, since the days are evil.

In the second place, it is also right to say that times are different, and that our people have a particular position and a particular opportunity precisely in our time and in our land to work for the life and freedom of the congregation. Precisely now, when we have moved out of the state church, it is a matter of making use of the opportune moment to find the proper form and manner of work of the free church.
