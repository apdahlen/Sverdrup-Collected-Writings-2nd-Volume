\section{The Centenary of Lay Work, 1796—1896}

[Source: “Folkebladet” for 1 January 1896. — Ed.]

This section appears on pages 289–291 of the original volume. — Present Ed.

It is impossible to write the new year-number—1896—without remembering that with it we begin the hundred-year jubilee of Norwegian lay work. For Norwegian lay work, with its unspeakable blessings for our people and our Church, has its origin in that remarkable hour in the history of our country when Hans Nielsen Hauge was called to be a voice crying among a people who, through the preaching of carnal pastors, were held in the sleep of death.

Hauge’s calling to be a preacher of God and a fisher of men took place on 5 April 1796. And this calling took place at the same time as “the breakthrough of the new life” in him. The Lord had long drawn him with love and fatherly goodness, and in the course of his twenty-fifth year his conversion and his seeking after the Lord had especially become a matter of earnestness.

Then it happened, on 5 April 1796, that he was out working in the fields and, while he worked, sang the hymn “Jesus, to Taste Your Sweet Communion.” He himself relates: “When I had sung the second verse, my mind was so lifted up to God that I was no longer conscious of myself, nor can I express what took place in my soul. The first thing of which I became conscious when my understanding gathered itself again was that I regretted that I had not served the dear God, who is good above all things; now it seemed to me that nothing in the world was worth regarding. It was a glory that no tongue can express; my soul felt something supernatural, divine, and blessed. I know all the good in my spirit that followed from that hour onward, especially the inward love toward God and my neighbor,—that I had an entirely changed mind, a sorrow over all sins, a desire that people should become partakers with me in the same grace, a particular delight in reading Holy Scripture, especially Jesus’ own teaching, together with new light t                   hat through his Spirit we should be born anew, turn, and be sanctified more and more according to the character of God, to serve the triune God alone, in order to ennoble and prepare our soul for eternal blessedness.”

“It was then as though I saw the world lying sunk in evil, over which I grieved greatly, and I prayed that God would withhold the punishment, so that some might turn. I now gladly wished to serve God and prayed that he would reveal to me what I should do.”

“I then heard the voice of the Lord, which said: Whom shall I send to invite to my great supper and to call from every side, north and south, east and west? I said: Send me; for my heart burned with love for all people.”

“It resounded within me: You shall confess my name before people, admonish them to turn and seek me while I may be found, to call upon me when I am near and touch their hearts, so that they might turn from darkness to light.”\footnote{A. Chr. Bang, Hans Nielsen Hauge og hans Samtid, pp. 21–22. — Ed.}

This is Hauge’s calling, which bears witness clearly enough that his whole work rests upon the living experience of salvation, that his calling to bear witness proceeds from his Christian standing and his standing as a child of God, not from a consciousness of some particularly great gift of grace that had to be put to use.

On this calling Hauge went. In faith in God and in the task that had been set before him, to invite people to come to God, he wandered through Norway from valley to valley. He was certain that the Lord had called him and sent him; therefore he could not rest so long as the Spirit drove him. No one can read the history of his life without feeling himself seized by the mighty faith that carried Hans Nielsen Hauge in his labor and his sufferings. He had been called to awaken the Norwegian people, and in the strength of the Lord he completed the work, which cost him everything—honor, freedom, health, and at last life itself.

This beautiful remembrance from the history of the Norwegian people speaks for itself. Let whoever will do otherwise. One does not honor one’s cause by praising Hauge and reviling his small and poor followers. For us it is not of the slightest value to build Hauge’s grave; but it is, on the contrary, a matter of life for our whole Church that it go forward in Hauge’s spirit, because it is a breath of the Spirit of God that through him has passed over our people. The graves of the prophets can well take care of themselves; but we do well when we “remember our guides and follow their faith, when we consider the outcome of their way of life.”

For a hundred years Norwegian lay work has been a work of love among the people, an earnest call from God to conversion and faith and salvation.

For a hundred years it has been a sign spoken against, which the humanists have despised, mocked, and persecuted.

For a hundred years it has been a calling of suffering; many of the pastors have rejected it, and the crude mob has cast curses and stones after it.

For a hundred years it has done its work for the awakening of many souls unto life; and while many of these awakened souls still strive for the crown on their pilgrimage toward eternity, there are also many of these awakened souls who have fought the fight to its end and won the victory and stand before the throne with palm branches in their hands.

A hundred years more,—where are we then? Yes, that depends upon what we are today and during the remainder of our days. A hundred years more,—where are the Norwegian people and the Norwegian Church then? Yes, that depends upon whether we know our time of visitation, whether we remain faithful in the calling. The Spirit of Christ entices and calls our people to follow the way of the Spirit and to live a life unto blessing for themselves and for our fellow human beings. The flesh draws and tempts us to sink down into sin and death, into worldliness and habitual Christianity, so that we shall lose our calling and become salt without strength, so that we are not even fit for manure upon the field. Shall the flesh prevail and we be lost, or shall the Spirit conquer, unto our eternal salvation?

“Wake up, and strengthen what remains, which is about to die!”

1

Jesus, to taste your sweet union,
My heart and my mind both long and press on;
Tear me from all that still holds me back,
Draw me into you, my Beginning!
Show me quite clearly my misery and trouble,
Show me the abyss of corruption in me,
That fallen nature may bow itself unto death,
And the spirit alone must live for you!

2

Strengthen me mightily within my soul,
That I may discover what the Spirit can do;
Take captive for yourself my speech and my mind,
Lead me and draw me, weak as I go!
Myself, and all that is mine, I gladly would lose,
If only you alone may dwell in the soul,
And at last whatever disturbs my inward rest
Must quietly slip out through the door.

3

O, if only one could learn the one thing:
To offer oneself up with heart and mind!
O, might Jesus alone become my all!
I am, alas, still far from it;
Jesus, who gave me an ear that can hear,
Reach out to me also your mighty hand,
That henceforth I may conduct my walk
Truly as a Christian, in a spirit of holiness!

4

Hear, O Jesus, your cooing dove;
Shepherd, seek out your wandering lamb!
Bear me among the myrrh a refreshing grape;
Cleanse my heart from sin and from shame!
Do not let me remain in the mere existence of the letter,
Which only outwardly makes one respectable and fine;
Let the Spirit inscribe the Law upon my heart,
That in truth I may call myself yours!

5

Jesus, when will you at last give me rest?
The burden presses hard—ah, take it from me!
When shall I see you smile upon me in kindness?
Rise up and rebuke the roaring sea!
Dearest Jesus, you must have mercy;
Do not hide your face forever from me!
Noblest wealth for those who are poor in spirit,
Fill my emptied heart with yourself!

6

Do not let me, O Jesus, cry out in vain;
See how hungry and weary the soul is!
Let us, Immanuel, be together;
If I have you alone, then I am satisfied!
Once you said: They would faint
If I sent them away hungry—
Eternal Love, can you then deny
Hungry souls even a little to eat?

7

Most gracious Jesus, now I will bind myself
To your precious covenant of promise:
Ask and seek, and you shall receive and find!
So your truthful lips themselves have said.
With the woman from the land of Canaan
I will cry after you, and I will not be silent,
Until at last you must set your seal upon the prayer:
Amen, yes, Amen: let it be to you as you will!
