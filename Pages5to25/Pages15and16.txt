STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* Menighed / Menigheden: carries ecclesiological weight beyond modern “congregation,” implying the church as a spiritual body.
* Hellighed: holiness as participation in God’s holiness, not moral respectability.
* Naademidlerne: technical Lutheran term (means of grace: Word and Sacraments).
* Bod: repentance with contrition, not merely apology.
* Vantro: unbelief, not simple doubt.
* Kirketugt: church discipline, not punishment but pastoral-ecclesial practice.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long cumulative sentences with causal and theological layering.
* Semicolon-driven argument flow.
* Embedded biblical quotations and allusions woven into prose.
* Rhetorical questions that must remain intact.

Theological Hazards:

* Relationship between holiness of the Church and holiness of its members.
* Role of Word and Sacraments as objective means of grace.
* Divine judgment beginning at God’s house.
* Sanctification as work of the Holy Spirit, not human achievement.

Voice Hazards:

* Earnest, exhortational Lutheran sermonic tone.
* Direct address to conscience of layperson and pastor.
* Moral urgency without emotional excess.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “Naadens Midler” – Lutheran confessional term; rendered as “means of grace.”
* “Alle mine Kilder er i dig” – Psalm 87:7; expresses total dependence on God as source of holiness.
* “begynder Dommen fra Guds Hus” – 1 Peter 4:17.
* “han, som banker” – Revelation 3:20.
* “det ufrugtbare Træ” – Luke 13:6–9.
* “Øksen er allerede lagt ved Roden” – Matthew 3:10.
* “opvokse til ham, som er Hovedet” – Ephesians 4:15.

Literal translation would weaken force if biblical resonance is lost; phrasing must echo Scripture naturally.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Correction of false view of congregational holiness.
* Positive definition: holiness grounded in God’s presence via means of grace.
* Explanation of judgment within the Church.
* Pastoral and lay implications.
* Defense against scandal caused by hypocrisy.
* Application to unconverted members.
* Application to clergy.
* Clarification of church discipline’s proper place.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain sober, warning tone.
* No mitigation of judgment imagery.
* Preserve exhortational pressure.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* “maa” → “must.”
* Conditional warnings retain inevitability.
* No weakening of necessity or obligation.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed → Church / Congregation (capitalized consistently).
* Hellighed → holiness.
* Naadens Midler → means of grace.
* Aand → Spirit / Holy Spirit.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* House / temple imagery retained.
* Vineyard and tree metaphors preserved.
* Living stones imagery preserved.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Warning → consolation → exhortation → clarification.
* No emotional flattening.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences preserved where theological reasoning accumulates.
* Clause rhythm respected.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

But this may suffice concerning the harm and confusion that arise from a distorted consideration of the Church’s holiness. Many could add much more to this matter from painful experiences that may be made daily.

The holiness of the Church must be believed, because it cannot be seen, and yet it is a true and living reality. The Church is holy not by its own holiness, but by God’s holiness, which is in it through the means of grace. It is an assembly of people who have dared to take up for their own use and for that of their fellow human beings the only holy thing that exists on earth: the Lord’s Word and Sacraments. This community is holy not because the people who constitute it are holy, but because the Lord is there with his Spirit and grace. He dwells in the Church through the Word and the Sacraments, and his house is a sanctuary. It is he himself who sanctifies the house. He himself is the sole source of holiness, and from him, through Word and Sacrament, holiness flows to the Church and to its individual members. If the Church rightly uses the means of grace in repentance and faith, with hearts opened to the gracious working of the Spirit, then the Spirit sanctifies it and by this his activity forms the body of Christ and himself builds the house that is joined together of living stones. Thus it is the holiness of the Church—namely, the Holy Spirit in Word and Sacrament—that makes the members holy; not the other way around. “All my springs are in you.”

But if some member of the Church does not use Word and Sacrament for salvation, does not open the door to him who knocks, then “judgment begins from the house of God”; for God’s house must be holy, and the Lord sanctifies himself and his house by judging those who will not allow themselves to be sanctified. Yet the Lord takes his time in holding judgment; he waits with patience even for the unfruitful tree in his vineyard and does not immediately cut it down; and he often waits long for the conversion of a member of the Church and does not immediately cut him off.

This is the faith of the saints. Thus we believe that the Church is holy, even though we see much unholiness among its members; for thus the Lord himself teaches us in his Word. Or how else could he himself say that judgment begins from the house of God, if there were no uncleanness in it, or if there were nothing but converted and true Christians in the house?

This consideration of the Church’s holiness alone is suited to give the proper fear and reverence for it among all its members. The one who wishes to be a true member of the Church does not judge its holiness by the holiness of other members, but by the holiness of Christ; and when he sees how much is lacking, he seeks refuge in the source of grace, in Word and Sacrament, in order through them to grow up to him who is the head. By this he is preserved in humility.

By this consideration of the Church’s holiness, the newly awakened will not be frightened away from the Church, even if he sees sin and unbelief among its members; for if God is there, and Christ, and the Holy Spirit, then there is holiness enough, and his prayer will be that he himself may nevertheless walk worthy of the calling with which he has been called.

By this consideration the full weight of responsibility can be laid upon the unconverted member of the Church. For he is the unfruitful tree in the Lord’s garden, and the axe is already laid at the root of the tree; if he does not repent, it is cut down and cast into the fire and burned.

By this consideration the pastor will be strengthened for vigorous work. For he will recognize that he is not without support in the very presence of the Lord in the Word and the Spirit. The holiness of the Lord’s house will fill him with zeal to seek to win all souls for their Savior and to warn all against the impending judgment. In the very holiness of the Church there is for him both responsibility and power. He is driven to labor with diligence for the edification of the Church and never to look through his fingers at its defects and shortcomings; and when he reproves and exhorts, it is not merely because he so earnestly desires to save souls, but also because the holiness of the Church demands of him that he not calmly look on while it is being defiled.

Thus a serious and Christian consideration of the Church’s holiness is suited to form a firm point of departure for the work in the Church and to give it the proper weight and the proper authority. But must then all sinful behavior be tolerated in the Church, and must no profane person be cast out of the Church?

Yes, there must indeed be church discipline; but not in order thereby to sanctify the Church. Church discipline has regard to other matters; it proceeds from the Church’s holiness, it does not bring the Church or its holiness into being. But of this, another time. First, other questions concerning the Church must be addressed.
