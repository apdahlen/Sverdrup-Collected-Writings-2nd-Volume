STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* Menighed / Menigheden: carries ecclesiological weight; must be rendered consistently as "congregation" or "church" with care.
* Søvndyssende / sjæledræbende: metaphorical, sermonic terms implying spiritual anesthesia and death.
* Salighed: not mere happiness; refers to eternal salvation/blessedness.
* Omvendelse / Bod: repentance in the strong biblical sense, not moral improvement.
* Aand / Aanden: Spirit; distinction between human spirit and the Holy Spirit must be preserved.
* Frihed: theological freedom (from coercion), not modern autonomy.
* Naadegaverne: charismata / gifts of grace.
* Hellighed: holiness as ecclesial quality, not moral perfection.

Syntactic Hazards

* Long sentences with causal connectors and semicolon logic.
* Parallel negations and repeated clauses building cumulative argument.
* Sermonic pacing that should not be broken into short modern sentences.

Theological Hazards

* Definition of the church grounded in Word and Sacraments.
* Distinction between visible membership and spiritual life.
* Danger of false security and false judgments of holiness.
* Ecclesial freedom versus external coercion.

Voice Hazards

* Earnest, admonitory Lutheran tone addressing conscience.
* Sharp warnings against spiritual sleep and pride.
* Rhetorical contrasts that must not be softened.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "Søvndyssende og sjæledræbende" – metaphor for teachings that lull conscience and extinguish spiritual life.
* "gaa ud" – allusion to Rev. 18:4; separationist language.
* "Babel" – biblical image of confusion and false unity (Gen. 11).
* "har et sterkt Gudfrygtigheds Skin" – appearance of piety without truth (cf. 2 Tim. 3:5).

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Clarification of what Scripture does and does not teach about the congregation.
* Identification of false, spiritually dangerous definitions of church.
* Positive presentation of a biblical, living, spiritual congregation.
* Consequences: freedom, activity, mission.
* Transition to the question of holiness.
* Refutation of the idea that holiness depends on members’ moral state.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Word and Sacraments as means of grace (Matt. 28:19–20; Acts 2:42).
* Call to repentance addressed to those within (Rev. 2–3).
* "Go out from her" – Revelation 18:4.
* Babel – Genesis 11.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Warnings against spiritual sleep and pride must remain sharp.
* Do not soften critique of false ecclesiology.
* Maintain earnest exhortational pressure.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* maa / kan / er ikke: preserve necessity and impossibility.
* Keep strong declarative judgments intact.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed = congregation
* Ordet og Sakramenterne = the Word and the Sacraments
* Salighed / Frelse = salvation
* Hellighed = holiness

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Sleep, death, sources, Babel imagery must remain vivid.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Contrast between warning and reassurance must be preserved.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Maintain long, flowing sentences suitable for proclamation.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

It is therefore not a sleep-inducing or soul-destroying doctrine that Scripture presents, when it shows us the congregation as a fellowship of those who use the Word and the Sacraments for their own salvation and that of other people, and when it shows us that the congregation can continue to be a congregation even though it has individual dead members. But it is true that all deviations from Scripture’s presentation of the congregation are apt to produce sleep and death.

It is sleep-inducing and misleading when one teaches that the congregation is an association for maintaining a pastor and a church and a burial ground. These things belong to the work of the congregation, but they are not the essence of the congregation. And a man can be a member of such a congregation without any other requirement resting upon him from the side of the congregation than that he pays his regular contributions. It is also sleep-inducing when one thinks that the congregation is a society for preserving and maintaining pure doctrine; for in that there lies no other demand upon the members of the congregation than that they procure for themselves an orthodox teacher. It is also sleep-inducing when one thinks that the congregation consists only of the converted, and that one must go outside the congregation in order to find the unconverted. For then the one who is inside the congregation becomes secure and confident, and he calmly lets all exhortation to repentance and conversion pass him by and sends it on to those who are outside.

If one wishes to work for a living and spiritual congregation, then one must take a definite stand in the Word of God, which presents to us living and spiritual congregations, in which the Word and the Sacraments, through the powerful working of God’s Spirit, are the living sources from which the members of the congregation receive faith and life and spirit, if they use them rightly.

This biblical understanding—that the congregation is a fellowship gathered by the Spirit and the Word, without any external legal compulsion or outward enticements, and that it uses the Word and the Sacraments for its own salvation and that of others—entails that congregations must be free, active, and engaged in mission.

A congregation that has not come into being in freedom has not come into being in the right way according to Scripture. A congregation that does not exercise freedom and put the gifts of grace to use does not order itself according to Scripture. A congregation that does not labor in mission does not use the Word and the Sacraments as God’s Word requires. But even though there are in this respect great weaknesses and grave failures, still not every mark of the congregation has yet disappeared.

From this consideration of the local congregation, which we believe is firmly grounded in the Lord’s Word, we proceed in the treatment of all questions concerning the congregation. If this point of departure is not biblical, and if we have erred here, then much will become erroneous in our understanding of the work and life of the congregation. We therefore gladly ask all who understand the matter to correct possible distortions at once. For nothing can be more dangerous or more harmful for all our free-church work than to spread distorted conceptions of the congregation, its essence, and its activity.

\subsubsection{On the Holiness of the Congregation}

The question is raised whether a congregation is holy when not all of its members are believing, converted, true, reborn Christians. And there is scarcely any point at which we hear so many harsh judgments about the congregations as precisely here. For it is an undeniable fact that many belong to the congregations who are not living Christians. One points to these and to their life and conduct, and from this one draws the conclusion that the congregation to which they belong is not holy. And from this one concludes again that it is not any congregation at all, but a Babel, from which reborn and living Christians must “go out.”

Such judgments arise from the thought that the holiness of the congregation rests upon the holiness of its members. According to this way of thinking, it is the holy people who make the congregation holy. If, then, a sufficient number of holy people unite, one gets a holy congregation; but in no other way does one get any holy congregation, and therefore, in truth, no congregation at all, but a Babel.

This way of thinking has a strong appearance of godliness and at the same time appeals, precisely for that reason, to the proud human heart; for it is indeed a great exaltation for the one who can thus be among those who sanctify the congregation.

Therefore this way of thinking is also well suited to cause a great deal of harm and confusion.
