STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* Hellighed / helliggjort: carries strong ecclesiological and doctrinal meaning (holiness as God-given, not moral perfection)
* Menighed: congregation/church as theological body, not merely social group
* Vanhellig / vanhellighed: desecration, profanation, not merely moral failure
* Vækkelse / nyvakte Sjæle: pietistic revival terminology
* Salighedssag: matter of salvation, soteriologically absolute
* Dømmesyge: judgmentalism with moral-spiritual connotation

Syntactic Hazards

* Extremely long causal sentences with layered conditional logic
* Sermonic progression with repeated “Thi” (for/because)
* Rhetorical escalation through accumulation rather than sharp breaks

Theological Hazards

* Doctrine of the church’s holiness independent of members’ sanctity
* Relationship between individual salvation and ecclesial belonging
* Danger of sectarianism and schism
* Pastoral theology regarding clergy authority and reverence for the church

Voice Hazards

* Solemn, admonitory Lutheran tone
* Earnest warnings against pride, self-righteousness, and schism
* Pastoral grief and lament must not be softened

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “Salt og Lys” – biblical idiom (Matthew 5) referring to preserving and illuminating influence
* “Babel” – Genesis 11; confusion, division, loss of common language
* “Kirkeflugtssag” – church flight/secession as spiritual crisis
* “holde sit Hoved høiere” – pride, self-exaltation

Literal translation risks flattening spiritual force; idioms retained with biblical resonance.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Thesis: The holiness of the congregation does not arise from the holiness of its members
* Argument 1: Such a view lowers the standard of holiness and weakens repentance
* Argument 2: It inevitably produces judgmentalism, schism, and sectarianism
* Argument 3: It harms newly awakened believers and weakens the congregation
* Argument 4: It corrupts the unconverted by removing reverence and responsibility
* Argument 5: It distorts pastoral ministry and undermines clerical authority

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Matthew 5:13–16 (Salt and Light)
* Genesis 11 (Babel)
* Implicit ecclesiology of 1 Corinthians and Ephesians (church as holy body)

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

Warnings, grief, and rebuke retained without mitigation or emotional smoothing.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

Necessity and inevitability (“maa”, “kan ikke”, “vilde”) preserved as strong modal claims.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed – congregation
* Hellighed – holiness
* Helliggjørelse – sanctification
* Salighed – salvation

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

Metaphors of stain, salt/light, Babel, trampling retained intact.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

Severity (warning) contrasted with pastoral sorrow and concern.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

Long sentences and sermonic rhythm preserved.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

First and foremost, this view is to great harm to a serious contemplation of holiness itself. For if the holiness of the congregation is produced by the holiness of its members, then there is not much to it. For even if a congregation were found that consisted only of living Christians, even such a congregation would not be sanctified by the holy conduct and fellowship of its members. For it would always be exceedingly deficient. One would then have to reduce the holiness of the congregation to a minimum, which would necessarily lead to dullness and slackness in the recognition of sin and in the struggle for sanctification, and this would be followed by a corresponding self-exaltation and self-satisfaction.

Next, this notion—that the holiness of the members sanctifies the congregation—is corrupting, because it necessarily leads to judgmentalism and strife and parties and divisions. For it is certainly true that the congregation must be holy, otherwise it is not a congregation at all, and then I cannot be saved without leaving it. If now the holiness of the congregation is to rest upon the holiness of the members, then it becomes for me, who wish to be saved, a matter of salvation to ascertain whether the other members of the congregation are holy. For from them the holiness of the congregation is supposed to come, and if now not all are holy, then either these unholy ones must be expelled from the congregation, or else I myself must leave it for the sake of my soul’s salvation. Thus begins the investigation into how it stands with the holiness of the other members. And it does not take long before one finds an unclean person among the people of the congregation. And after a while one finds another, and then yet another, and soon one sees nothing but sheer hypocrites all around. If one then finds some like-minded persons, the thought soon matures that these few, whom we know as our own, they are the true congregation; and if they cannot expose the others, then they must separate themselves, and thus one soon has the whole party-spirit with judgmentalism and heart-hostility in full bloom. One case of church flight follows after another; one division succeeds another, and amid the steadily growing confusion the congregation easily becomes the very “Babel” one wished to avoid, a home of strife where one does not understand the language of the other.

Furthermore, this view does harm in that it often drives newly awakened souls to leave a congregation rashly. They are awakened, they come to themselves and look about. They find unholiness in the conduct and fellowship of the members of the congregation; they are horrified and think: here is not God’s congregation. It is best to flee from here. If they then leave the congregation because it is not holy enough, they have thereby often done themselves much harm, and they have always deprived the congregation of the spiritual strength that was meant to benefit it and which the Lord had given them, that they might be salt and light also within the congregation. They themselves may well suffer loss thereby; the congregation will always suffer loss thereby. And sorrowful and heart-rending it often is, when God’s children in a congregation and a faithful shepherd of souls have perhaps for a long time sighed and prayed that awakening might come in the congregation, then to have to watch as the newly awakened leave the congregation, and its chamber becomes larger instead of smaller.

Furthermore, this view—that the congregation is only as holy as its members make it—does great harm to the unconverted members of the congregation. They lose their respect for the congregation; they feel no responsibility in belonging to it. They are unconcerned that their sinful life is a stain upon God’s sanctuary, for they reckon that the congregation is no holier than they themselves are, and in their crude minds they do not grasp their own shame. For they soon imagine that they are good enough for the company in which they find themselves. And when no one reminds them that the congregation is holier than all its members taken together, it is no wonder if they hold their heads higher and trample the holiness of the congregation underfoot; and if there are some poor children of God in the congregation, they try to find as many blemishes in them as possible in order to reassure themselves thoroughly that they are perfectly holy enough for the company.

Finally, only one more thing shall be mentioned here, although much could be added, and that is the harm this view does by often leading pastors to regard their congregations only as a mission field. And it is indeed true and certain that the pastor who does not regard his congregation as a mission field is a very poor pastor; but on the other hand, if he sees the congregation only in this light, then he does not nurture the respect for its holiness and the reverence for God’s house which alone can give him the right point of departure for his work toward the congregation. The pastor, more than anyone else, needs to be thoroughly permeated with the conviction that the congregation is holy; but if he judges the holiness of the congregation by the holiness of its members, then he is soon tempted to despise the congregation and becomes dulled in his perception of the claim which the congregation places upon him and which he places upon the congregation. If the pastor regards himself as standing outside and above the congregation because he considers it so imperfect and unholy, then his influence and spiritual authority in the congregation will soon be at an end.
