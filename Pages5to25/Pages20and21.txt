STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "Menighed" used in a strong ecclesiological sense (congregation/church body), not merely "parish".
* "Verdslighed" denotes spiritual worldliness, not neutral secularity.
* "Samfund" refers to church bodies/synods, not society at large.
* "Aand" carries theological weight as Spirit, often implicitly the Holy Spirit.
* "Kristelig" vs. "kristen" distinctions must be preserved.
* "Embedsmands Skik" refers to Norwegian state-official lifestyle and ethos.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Very long sentences with cumulative logic.
* Semicolon-based reasoning and rhetorical pacing.
* Frequent abstract nouns governing long dependent clauses.
* Rhetorical questions embedded in argument.

Theological Hazards:

* Distinction between outward church membership and true Christianity.
* Repentance, faith, bondage, freedom, Spirit vs. world.
* Ecclesiology: visible vs. living congregation.
* Priesthood of all believers.

Voice Hazards:

* Earnest, admonitory, prophetic tone.
* Appeals to conscience must not be softened.
* Strong warnings against self-deception and false security.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "holde dem i godt Lag og Lune" – idiom meaning to keep people pleased and content; spiritually implies pandering.
* "Verdslighedens Aag" – yoke of worldliness; biblical imagery (Matt. 11; Gal. 5).
* "Mare" – nightmare/incubus; metaphor for oppressive delusion.
* "Lys af Kjærlighed" – likely echo of John 13:35; love as mark of God’s children.
* "Trællene" – slaves/servants; biblical contrast with sons (John 8; Gal. 4).

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Identification of two foundational errors in church life.
* Description of American contextual pressures.
* Warning about numerical and financial pragmatism.
* Diagnosis of resulting worldliness and spiritual paralysis.
* Call for honest recognition of true spiritual condition.
* Emphasis on clear distinction between believers and unbelievers.
* Vision of a freed, Spirit-filled, active congregation.
* Rejection of legal/constitutional solutions apart from Spirit.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Spirit vs. world – 1 Corinthians 2:12.
* Children vs. slaves – John 8:35; Galatians 4.
* Love as identifying mark – John 13:34–35.
* Freedom by the Spirit – 2 Corinthians 3:17.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain sharp warnings and exhortations.
* Preserve moral urgency and prophetic clarity.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* "maa", "kan", "vil" retain necessity and impossibility.
* Avoid weakening obligation into suggestion.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed = congregation
* Verdslighed = worldliness
* Aand = Spirit
* Frihed = freedom
* Trældom = bondage

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Yoke, bondage, light, slavery, steam engine metaphors preserved intact.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Severity in diagnosis; hope in Spirit-given freedom.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences retained where argument accumulates.
* Sermonic flow preserved.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

These two great errors are closely connected; for a settlement congregation without an understanding of the nature and proper character of the congregation, and a dignified and solemn pastor who lives according to the Norwegian civil‑servant custom, in truth belong together.

But to this state‑church character there are added the particular difficulties under which we labor in America. The small, beginning congregations are placed in great temptations and under severe trials. Many fall, and not a few remain fallen.

For the matter is this: one must become as many as possible in the congregation. Money is required for a church, money for a pastor, money for a parochial school, money for a seminary, money for the synod fund, money for missions. Men are needed who can contribute something, preferably something substantial. What, then, is to be done? First, to get as many as possible into the congregation; second, to keep them in good humor and disposition once they have been brought in.

But in this way one gains a worldly and fateful beginning for a congregation, and this crooked beginning often binds the congregation and paralyzes it in its work for long periods of time.

It would lead too far to enumerate all the temptations to worldliness that lie along the congregation’s path; we shall only add that almost everywhere the fearless testimony of faith from the Christians in the congregation is lamentably weak. So restrained do the Christians become, because of the many considerations that must be taken toward the world in and around the congregation, that in many congregations one is compelled to ask: Where, then, are the Christians in this congregation?

It is from this layer of worldliness that our congregations need deliverance.

For this there is need of a proclamation—by pastor or layperson—that goes more directly to the very heart of the matter and to the actual condition of the congregations than is customary.

There is need for the yoke of bondage that lies over so many congregations and souls to be torn off and removed, by its being acknowledged that no congregation is Christian simply because it bears the name, and no human being is Christian simply because he belongs to a congregation. This delusion is a nightmare over the spiritual life in so many places. Why not come forth into the freedom of sincerity and truth, which consists in seeing and acknowledging things precisely as they are? Deliverance from self‑deception is indeed an opening of the mind that is exceedingly valuable.

Closely connected with this is the matter that the distinction between converted and unconverted, believing and unbelieving, becomes more clearly recognizable in the congregation and its work. The yoke of worldliness that lies upon the believers in our congregations—out of consideration upon consideration, and yet again consideration—must be shattered.

There must arise a pure and clear testimony from the mouths of the believers, so that it can be perceived that they no longer have the spirit of the world, but the Spirit that is from God. There must arise a visible congregation, recognizable by the “light of love,” which is the proper mark and distinguishing sign of God’s children.

Work must be directed toward the goal that it be recognized that the church’s working force is not the pastors alone, but all believing men and women in the congregation—and the pastors only insofar as they themselves are believers. There is need of a whole and complete Christian activity, embracing both the work of pastors and that of the laity.

Let us be few, but let us be true; then we are also free. Only God’s true children are entitled to carry out the Father’s will. Slaves, to be sure, can perform work, but not in the right manner and not with the right spirit.

There will never be free congregations until there are living congregations. Only the Spirit of God can free from the yoke of worldliness and outward churchliness. But when the Spirit is present in the congregations, freedom comes of itself. Then the bondage under synodical domination will also fall away.

\subsubsection{A freed congregation}

Without Spirit there is no freedom in church or congregation. To suppose that one can produce freedom in the congregation by means of laws and constitutions is a delusion or an error of the same kind as if a man were to imagine that he could obtain steam power merely by possessing a steam boiler, or that he could obtain wheat without limit merely by having a threshing machine. A good constitution and good laws are useful when freedom is present; but if freedom is not in the congregation, then the law cannot produce it. Law does not create freedom; but if it is the right kind of law, it serves to give freedom opportunity to work and can even serve to preserve and protect freedom.
