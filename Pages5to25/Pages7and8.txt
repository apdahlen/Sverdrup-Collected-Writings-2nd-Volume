STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "fanatiske og usunde Retninger" – carries polemical-theological weight, not merely psychological.
* "Menighedsfagen" – technical ecclesial term (the question of the congregation), not a casual issue.
* "Frikirken" – historically specific Free Church movement.
* "Livsspørgsmaal" – existential, not rhetorical.
* "Stiftelse" – foundation/establishment with ecclesial overtones.
* "Børnelærdom" – catechism, not children’s learning in general.
* "Stedmenigheder / lokale Menigheder" – technical NT ecclesiology.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long, cumulative sentences with concessive and causal clauses.
* Semicolon pivots that must be preserved.
* Sermonic rhythm and logical buildup.

Theological Hazards:

* Doctrine of the church, congregation, and membership.
* Authority, discipline, and spiritual legitimacy.
* NT imagery: Body, Temple, Bride of Christ.

Voice Hazards:

* Earnest, exhortational Lutheran-pietistic tone.
* Polemical warning against false teaching.
* Collective address implicating the reader.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "bane sig Vej" – to force or make one’s way; metaphor of invasion.
* "tage det deri" – to derive one’s view from.
* "hænge sammen" – organic unity, not mere organization.
* "høie og hellige Navn" – biblical reverence language.
* NT echoes best matched with restrained, formal English rather than idiomatic modern phrasing.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Warning against unhealthy external influences.
* Justification for entering the debate.
* Assertion of the supreme importance of the congregational question.
* Consequences of strong vs. weak congregations.
* National and intergenerational significance.
* Necessity of clarity before answering practical questions.
* Turn to Scripture and the first Pentecost congregation.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Acts 2:41–42 explicitly quoted.
* Imagery: Temple of God, Body of Christ, Bride of Christ (Pauline).

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Warnings retained without mitigation.
* Collective responsibility emphasized.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* "maa" rendered as must.
* Conditional inevitability preserved.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed = congregation.
* Kirke = church.
* Stiftelse = establishment/founding.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Planting/rooting imagery preserved.
* Organic unity metaphors retained.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Severity in warning, elevation in vision, sobriety in appeal.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences retained where argument accumulates.
* Measured pacing suitable for proclamation.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

In addition, there are signs that fanatical and unhealthy tendencies from outside will make their way in among us. The unsound views for which Pastor Trandberg and his Reformed kindred spirits act as spokesmen easily give rise to confusion, so long as no public exchange of views takes place concerning this very fundamental question. And therefore it is to be hoped that, if the congregational question first comes under discussion, many will take their understanding of it from those who have had far better opportunity to reflect upon the matter than we have. Yet someone must speak the first word; and thus this, for lack of something better, may serve as a small introduction.

These lines may also be excused by the fact that an exposition of the congregational question has in fact been requested in Folkebladet, so that it is not unprompted that we take up a matter which we clearly see to be both too great and too weighty for our abilities and our time, but which we hope others will take an interest in elucidating in a manner we ourselves do not command.

What must be correctly emphasized this time is the great significance of the matter. If the congregations are rightly planted and rightly rooted, then all other ecclesiastical work becomes relatively easy, solid, and secure. But if the congregations themselves are loose and unstable in their composition, then so much strength is wasted merely in holding the congregations together that there is scarcely time or energy left for any ther genuine work. Therefore, for the Free Church, which cannot thrive without progress and growth, it is a matter of life to have congregations formed that truly correspond to their high and holy name. And if this great task could be rightly solved, it would be of great and lasting benefit for us and our descendants. It would also be of extraordinary importance for Norway if the nature and work of the congregation were rightly and vividly set forth among us; for it will not be long before the same work which we are now doing here must also be done there, under far more difficult conditions than those with which we have to contend.

Therefore the matter is serious and great; and not least does it have such wide-reaching significance because all members of the congregation, men and women, old and young, must take part in the solution of the task if it is to be well accomplished. All therefore need to have a clear view of the congregation and a sure grasp of the work that must be done if the congregation is to receive its rightful establishment among us.

\subsubsection{What is the congregation?}

It is necessary for anyone who wishes to have a secure point of departure for the treatment of the many questions that stand in connection with the congregation first to form a clear understanding of what the congregation is.

Such questions as these: Who ought to be admitted into the congregation? Who ought to be expelled from the congregation? What demands does the congregation make upon its members? What is the pastor’s right in the congregation?—and many more questions which can and must both arise and be answered among us—can only be answered in a clear and satisfactory manner when it stands firm and clear before us what the congregation itself is.

Our catechism speaks of what the Church is, but not directly of what the congregation is. All the more does the New Testament speak of the congregation or the congregations. Now these are sometimes called parish congregations or “local congregations”; and it is not altogether free from a certain aftertaste of childishness when one speaks of “the local congregations.” We therefore permit ourselves to note that here we are speaking of “the local congregation.” And just as it is an incontrovertible fact that the New Testament speaks with the deepest reverence and holiest seriousness precisely of the local congregations and calls them God’s congregations and God’s temple and Christ’s body and Christ’s bride, so it is not too much that we also, with all reverence and respect, speak of this divine institution among us and esteem it very highly for Christ’s and God’s sake.

In order to obtain a secure answer to the question of what the congregation is, we may just as well at once go straight back to the first congregation, the one that was formed on the feast of Pentecost through the outpouring of the Spirit and Peter’s preaching. Of this event we read:

“Those who gladly received Peter’s word were baptized, and there were added that same day about three thousand souls to the congregation. And they continued steadfastly in the apostles’ teaching and the fellowship, in the breaking of bread and the prayers.”
