STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "Menighed" as both congregation/church; must avoid modern sociological "church."
* "Aanden" referring to the Holy Spirit with experiential connotations.
* "Konstitution" not in modern political sense but formal constitution.
* "Embedsmænd" as ecclesial office-holders.
* "Samfund" meaning fellowship/communion, not society.
* "Brødsbrydelsen" as the Lord’s Supper.
* "Salighed" as salvation/blessedness.
* "Sover" metaphorically for spiritual deadness (and possibly physical death in 1 Cor 11).

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long cumulative sentences with semicolons and logical pivots.
* Repeated causal connectors (thi, derfor).
* Sermonic pacing that must not be broken into short sentences.

Theological Hazards:

* Definition of the church grounded in Word and Sacraments, not subjective faith alone.
* Distinction between true church and perfect church.
* Objective means of grace vs. subjective state of members.

Voice Hazards:

* Earnest Lutheran polemic against perfectionism.
* Exhortational address to reader’s conscience.
* Didactic yet pastoral tone.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "lagdes den samme Dag til Menigheden" – Acts 2:41; passive divine action.
* "holde fast ved Apostlernes Undervisning" – Acts 2:42; perseverance.
* "store sorte Pletter" – vivid metaphor for moral/spiritual blemish.
* "en hel Hob sover" – 1 Cor 11:30; spiritual lethargy/death.
* "det siger sig selv" – rhetorical marker of logical inevitability.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Historical observation (Pentecost church existed prior to organization).
* Definition of church from Scripture.
* Extension to all apostolic churches.
* Clarification of organization as secondary.
* Formal definition: Word and Sacraments constitute the church.
* Objection: Is not true faith the mark?
* Scriptural rebuttal.
* Distinction between true and perfect church.
* Practical implication and exhortation.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Acts 2:41–42 (Pentecost, teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, prayers).
* 1 Corinthians 1:5; 11:30.
* Revelation 2–3 (Ephesus, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Laodicea).

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain firmness against subjectivism.
* Retain rebuke toward careless readers of Scripture.
* Preserve pastoral seriousness.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* "maa," "kan," "ophører" rendered with full necessity and consequence.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed = congregation/church.
* Ordet og Sakramenterne = the Word and the Sacraments.
* Omvendelse = repentance/conversion.
* Tro = faith.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Preserve imagery of stains, sleep, nourishment, preservation.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Severity in warning; consolation in God’s faithfulness to imperfect churches.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Retain long sentences and rhetorical flow suited for proclamation.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

The congregation was thus already present at the feast of Pentecost. It had not adopted any constitution, had not chosen officers, had not organized itself in any outward way by regulations and resolutions; and yet it was there, for the three thousand souls “were added that same day to the congregation.”

It is therefore simple enough, from this Word of God, to obtain an answer to the question: What is the congregation? For it can be nothing else than, first, the gathering of those upon whom the Spirit had fallen with visible and readily recognizable marks, and then all those who, through Peter’s preaching, believed, were baptized, and held fast to the apostles’ teaching, the fellowship, the breaking of the bread, and the prayers.

It was no different with all the other congregations which were gradually established through the labor and preaching of the apostles and other believing people. These too were formed through the working of the Spirit, the Word, and Baptism, and they continued by making use of the Word and the Supper.

It goes without saying that before long a fixed organization also came into use in all congregations, largely uniform everywhere; yet each congregation was organized for itself, without being bound together with other congregations by outward law and order, for they were bound together by the Holy Spirit.

From this it will be understood that when the Word of God speaks of congregations, it speaks of such fellowships as in each place were gathered by the Spirit and the Word, Baptism and the Supper, and which adopted an order precisely for the sake of these things—that is, ordered themselves with the purpose of having the proclamation of the Word and the use of the Sacraments in their midst, in order thereby to save themselves and to save others with them. They were fellowships of such as, themselves converted and believing, also wished to labor for the conversion and faith of other people by the means which the Lord had given.

Therefore it is the use of the Word and the Sacraments which constitutes a congregation in the biblical sense. It is the mark and distinguishing sign of the congregation and of its members that they use the Word and Baptism and the Supper for their own salvation and blessedness and that of other people.

But, one might perhaps ask: Is not faith, true and living faith, the real mark of the congregation? Can it be called a congregation when it does not consist solely of believers or living Christians? To this the Word of God gives an altogether definite and unambiguous answer.

The congregation remains a congregation even when some of its members are hypocrites, dead, sleeping, unbelieving, and ungodly. There may be great black stains upon a congregation, and yet it does not cease to be a congregation so long as it preserves the use of the Word and the Sacraments.

It may be a poor congregation, but it does not cease to be a congregation. Yes, it may, with many sleeping members, nevertheless be a true and genuine congregation of God.

If someone cannot read this in the Word of God, then at any rate he does not read it correctly. For the congregation in Corinth was a congregation of God, “rich in everything, in all teaching and all knowledge”; and yet Paul writes of it that “many among you are weak and sick, and a whole number are sleeping.” And for those among us who read the Word of God with seriousness, it is almost superfluous to mention what stands in John’s Revelation concerning the congregations in Ephesus and Pergamum and Thyatira and Sardis and Laodicea—how much was lacking in these congregations in faith, in life, in love, in zeal, and yet they were congregations, precious and beloved for Christ’s sake.

Therefore there is a difference between a true congregation and a perfect congregation, just as there is a difference between a sincere Christian and a perfect Christian. If we will follow the Word of God in its guidance concerning the congregation—which alone can give us reliable guidance in this matter—then we must say that it does not abolish the congregation if it has individual dead members; but it does abolish the congregation when it abolishes the use of the Word and the Sacraments.

The Word and the Sacraments must be used, and they must be used according to their divine appointment. They must be used in order to work repentance and faith both within and outside the congregation, used to preserve, strengthen, nourish, and sustain faith and life in the congregation. Whoever therefore neglects the hearing of the Word and the use of the Sacraments thereby separates himself from the congregation and gradually ceases altogether to be a member of the congregation.

It is therefore self-evident that not all members of the congregation are saved; but it is just as self-evident that it is the calling of the congregation to labor with the purpose and the prayer that, if possible, all its members might become converted, regenerated, and saved.
