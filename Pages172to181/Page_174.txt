STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "Kirkeorden" – not merely "church order" in a generic sense, but a historically loaded term referring to ecclesial constitution, polity, and practical ordering of church life.
* "Tilsigelse" – carries juridical and theological nuance (formal assurance, promise, or authoritative declaration), not a casual "statement".
* "Menighed" – congregation as a theological entity, not simply a local assembly.
* "Vækkelse" / "Søvn" – revival and spiritual sleep, pietistic terms with strong evaluative force.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long conditional sentences with cumulative logic.
* Repeated anaphoric "Hvis … saa" constructions that build rhetorical pressure.
* Sermon-like pacing that must not be broken into modern short sentences.

Theological Hazards:

* Distinction between the New Testament congregation and later church constitutions.
* Authority of God’s Word versus ecclesiastical arrangements.
* Concepts of living vs. dead congregations, revival vs. spiritual sleep.

Voice Hazards:

* Polemical, earnest, and ironic tone.
* Implied rebuke of ecclesiastical authority figures.
* Rhetorical confidence that must not be softened.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "det saa igrunden lidt vanskeligt ud" – idiomatic understatement; conveys mounting difficulty rather than mild inconvenience.
* "ingen Sag at bevise" – ironic minimization; implies the claim is trivially easy only if one accepts the premise.
* "forsnakket sig" – implies careless or revealing speech, not merely "misspoke".
* "gammel Vin" – biblical allusion (Matt. 9:17); here used polemically.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Presentation of a dilemma: either the old church order equals the NT congregation or it does not.
* Demonstration that both horns of the dilemma are problematic for defenders of the old order.
* Introduction of a new defensive claim by Pastor Kildahl.
* Systematic refutation by showing inconsistency with prior statements and silence.
* Clarification of what the disputed term actually refers to in context.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Implicit reliance on sola Scriptura: "Guds Ord nok".
* "gammel Vin" – echo of Christ’s teaching on new wine and old wineskins.
* Living vs. dead congregations – echoes Revelation 3.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Preserve irony and firmness.
* Retain accusatory force without exaggeration.
* Maintain the sense of public theological accountability.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* "havde ment" / "havde vidst" – counterfactual force must remain strong.
* Preserve logical necessity implied in the repeated conditionals.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Kirkeorden – "church order" (consistently).
* Konstitution – "constitution".
* Menighed – "congregation".
* Ordets Forkyndelse – "preaching of the Word".
* Sakramenternes Forvaltning – "administration of the sacraments".

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Living vs. dead congregations.
* Awakening vs. sleep.
* Old wine imagery.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Ironic dismissal versus sober clarification.
* Polemical exposure followed by doctrinal seriousness.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Preserve parallel "If … then" constructions.
* Maintain cumulative rhetorical rhythm.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (VOICE-PRESERVING)

At this point the discussion had put the old church order in a difficult position; for if it were the same as the congregation of the New Testament, then the Word of God would indeed be sufficient, and this assurance entirely superfluous. But if it were something other than the congregation of the New Testament, then this was highly unfortunate for the church order and for its defenders.

It now appears that at least Pastor Kildahl in Chicago is not satisfied with the situation that thus emerged. He therefore comes forward in the latest issue of “The Lutheran” with a new defense of the old church order; and he now asserts that the old church order is nothing other than the constitution of the United Church. And then it is no great challenge to prove that the constitution of the United Church, with its one-sided rule by majority, is altogether excellent.

Pastor Kildahl has admittedly remained silent for quite some time before coming forward with this information. And some time was indeed required before such a claim could even be believed among the people of the majority. For even if Pastor Kildahl himself believes the claim, that does not make it true.

If Chairman Høyme had meant the constitution of the United Church and the form of church governance determined therein, then he would surely have had the ability to say so himself.

If the annual meeting of the United Church last year had meant the constitution, then this meeting too would surely have had sufficient ability to state that the United Church continued to uphold its constitution.

If “The Lutheran” last year had known that the old church order was nothing other than the constitution of the body, then “The Lutheran” would have said so last year.

If Dr. Schmidt had arrived at the thought that the old church order was the constitution, then he would not have misspoken, whether about “old wine” or about many other matters.

The matter is this: Chairman Høyme speaks of the old church order in connection with the question of living or dead congregations, and in connection with the question of the preaching of the Word and the administration of the sacraments in the congregation, and in connection with the question of awakening or sleep in the congregations.
