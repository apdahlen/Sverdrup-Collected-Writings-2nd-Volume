STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* "Kirkeorden" – not merely "church order" administratively, but the historically inherited ecclesiastical system with liturgical, institutional, and spiritual implications.
* "Menighedens Liv og Livskilder" – life of the congregation and its sources of life; not sociological vitality but spiritual life and means of grace.
* "udvortes Partistilling" – outward party alignment; refers to external factional positioning, not merely opinion.
* "forfærdelig Hindring" – must retain strong moral weight; not simply "difficulty" or "problem".
* "frikirkelig" – free‑church in a theological–ecclesiological sense, not generic independence.

Syntactic Hazards

* Long cumulative sentences with repeated "Saaledes" establishing argumentative parallelism.
* Heavy use of causal connectors and inferential logic that must not be simplified.
* Embedded references to reports, meetings, and publications that function rhetorically as evidence.

Theological Hazards

* "Menighedslivet" and "aandelige Liv" – spiritual life as wrought by God, not emotional vitality.
* "fri" – spiritual and ecclesial freedom with conscience implications.
* The contrast between outward order and inward life must remain doctrinally sharp.

Voice Hazards

* Polemical yet restrained tone: earnest, sober, ecclesial.
* Moral seriousness without sarcasm.
* Corporate voice (“we”) expressing pastoral judgment.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "den aller nærmeste Forbindelse" – strongest possible connection; emphatic.
* "i streng Forstand saakaldte kirkelige Skik" – so‑called church custom in the strict sense; mildly ironic distancing.
* "et godt Mærke" – a good sign; moral–spiritual indicator.
* "gjør sig saa megen Umage" – makes such great effort; implies defensiveness.
* "blir fri" – becomes free; echoes Pauline spiritual freedom.

No direct biblical quotations, but strong resonance with Pauline freedom and life vs. form contrasts.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Assertion: The committee and others understood the chairman’s words as relating directly to congregational life.
* Corroboration: Multiple respected voices interpret "the old church order" the same way.
* Appeal to evidence: Directs reader to a specific report page.
* Concession: Acknowledges Kildahl does not defend the old church order in that strict sense.
* Core claim: All true Christians feel the old church order hinders spiritual life.
* Psychological inference: Kildahl’s insistence on being free‑church arises from this awareness.
* Conclusion: Affirmation and guarded approval of genuine free‑church freedom.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Implicit echo of Galatians (freedom vs. bondage).
* Johannine and Pauline contrast of life vs. dead form.
* Pastoral appeal to conscience rather than authority.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Retain sharp judgment: "terrible hindrance."
* Maintain communal lament and seriousness.
* Avoid ironic softening.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* Preserve certainty in statements of fact and shared conviction.
* Do not weaken declarative assertions into opinions.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Kirkeorden – "church order"
* Menighed – "congregation"
* Menighedsliv – "congregational life"
* aandeligt Liv – "spiritual life"
* frikirkelig – "free‑church"

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* "Hindring" as obstruction, not inconvenience.
* "Livskilder" as sources of life; keep metaphorical force.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Balance critique with final expression of joy and hope.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Preserve repeated sentence openings and rhythmic accumulation.
* Maintain long sentences where argument unfolds.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

The matter is this: the Committee also understood the Chairman’s statement, on the basis of his report, as standing in the closest connection with the question of the congregation’s life and its sources. The same understanding was shared by the few men who, at last year’s annual meeting, objected to the expression “the old church order.” In this same way Dr. Schmidt and *The Lutheran* have expressed themselves the entire time, so that one can see that they are thinking of congregational life and congregational formation—the so-called ecclesiastical custom in the strict sense—when there is talk of “the old church order.”

Therefore, if Pastor Kildahl carefully reads page 18 of the United Church’s Annual Report for 1897, he will rightly see what is at issue here.

It is, however, a good sign that Pastor Kildahl does not defend the old church order in the sense in which Chairman Høyme, the annual meeting, and J. A. S. speak of it.

For whether one stands on one side or the other in the outward party divisions, this is certain: that all Christian people, and especially all Christian pastors in our congregations, feel keenly that “the old church order” is a terrible hindrance to spiritual life.

It is presumably out of this very feeling that Pastor Kildahl makes such great effort to show that he and his are the truly free‑church people.

We have always believed that Pastor Kildahl, despite all his curious newspaper articles, was in his heart on the right side in the main matter. We rejoice over everyone who in truth becomes free and free‑church.
