STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "vanhellige Alliance" – polemical phrase; must retain sense of profanation and illegitimate union.
* "Verdsligheden" – not merely 'worldliness' in moral sense, but secular power and control over church life.
* "Præstevælde" – clerical domination; not neutral 'clergy leadership'.
* "Menighed" – congregation as spiritual body, not administrative parish.
* "Lægmandsvirksomheden" – lay ministry/lay activity with ecclesial weight.
* "Storehøigen" – metaphor; unclear modern reference, likely folkloric or proverbial image of a large, obstructive mound.
* "uflaadelig Kamp" – unceasing/unappeasable struggle; must not be weakened.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long periodic sentences with multiple metaphoric clauses.
* Sermonic contrasts (calm times vs. times of seriousness).
* Rhetorical escalation that should not be broken into short sentences.

Theological Hazards:

* Opposition of Life vs. Death as spiritual realities.
* Church defined by Christ’s words, not institutional peace.
* Liberation of congregational life from secular yoke as theological necessity.

Voice Hazards:

* Polemical, exhortational Lutheran tone.
* Direct moral pressure on reader.
* Sharp metaphors (dragon, serpent, poison).

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "den gamle Kirkeorden" – ironic designation; implies false sanctification of corrupt system.
* Dragon/serpent imagery – biblical symbolism of satanic opposition (Rev. 12; Gen. 3).
* "I er alle Brødre" – Matthew 23:8.
* "den største blandt eder skal være alles Tjener" – Matthew 23:11 / Mark 9:35.
* Boat, waves, rowing – ecclesial progress under persecution (cf. Mark 4:37–40).
* "Dødens Ro" – peace without struggle equals spiritual death.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Thesis: The so‑called old church order is the congregation’s most dangerous enemy.
* Development: In calm times it obstructs progress quietly; when challenged, it becomes violently hostile.
* Historical progression: Struggles over lay ministry, pulpit authority, and now the congregation itself.
* Present diversity: Different alignments among clergy and congregations.
* Teleology: Movement toward genuine congregational life under Christ’s teaching.
* Exhortation: Do not lose heart; resistance indicates progress.
* Warning: Peace through passivity equals death.
* Final appeal: Victories are meaningless unless they free congregational life from secular domination.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Matthew 23:8 – "You are all brothers."
* Matthew 23:11 – "The greatest among you shall be your servant."
* Revelation/Genesis imagery – dragon, serpent, poison.
* Life vs. Death – Johannine and Pauline dualism.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain sharp contrasts and warnings.
* Preserve polemical imagery without dilution.
* Retain urgency and moral pressure.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* Preserve necessity in "maa," "bør," implicit obligation.
* Do not weaken exhortative questions.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* Menighed = congregation.
* Verdslighed = secular domination.
* Præstevælde = clerical domination.
* Liv/Død = spiritual life/death.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Preserve dragon, serpent, waves, boat, yoke imagery.
* Avoid explanatory paraphrase.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Maintain shifts between struggle, hope, warning, exhortation.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Preserve long sentences and sermonic pacing.
* Maintain rhetorical rhythm suitable for proclamation.

STEP 11: FINAL TRANSLATION

This unholy alliance, which, as has been said, has from a certain quarter been adorned with the name “the old church order,” is of course the congregation’s most dangerous enemy. In times of calm it is like the “Great Burial Mound,” something heavy, slimy, and impassable, which everywhere presses against light and progress. But if anyone seriously demands the reality of the congregation where the name of congregation exists, then the dragon hisses and the serpent strikes and spews its poison over the striving combatant, however insignificant he may be.

Yet the pastor is not always willing to bow to worldliness; nor is the congregation always willing to bow to clerical rule. Therefore the struggle assumes different forms and manifests itself in different ways. From this there has arisen among us the struggle over lay ministry, over the pulpit, and now most recently over the congregation itself. These are, after all, only different stages in the same development.

The battle positions are therefore also quite varied. There are pastors who manfully fight against clerical domination and worldliness, just as there are congregations that with visible satisfaction submit to both. And the reverse as well. But beneath all this the conflicts continue, and we are steadily moving forward toward a genuine congregational life, where the words of Jesus are taken seriously: “You are all brothers,” and “the greatest among you shall be the servant of all.”

Therefore this is no time to lose heart. It is moving forward; and the speed can be measured by the waves of resistance that crash against the boat. That signifies progress. Pull in the oars and drift with the current, and soon the waves will subside and the noise cease. That is the calm of death.

Yet the struggle is not always equally hard, even though it is an unceasing struggle, as long as life and death contend with one another. It is not only a matter of the congregation advancing; it is also a matter of its being preserved. The latter is no less than the former.

The victories that have thus far been won in our ecclesial work through the recognition of lay ministry and Christian pastoral formation are of subordinate importance if they are not followed by, and do not bear their fruit in, the liberation of congregational life from the yoke of secular domination.

Is there not here something to labor for? Ought not all Christians to take part in this work?
